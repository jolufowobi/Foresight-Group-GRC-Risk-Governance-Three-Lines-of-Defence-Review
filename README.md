# Foresight Group | GRC Risk Governance & Three Lines of Defence Review

## 1. Overview

This case study examines the risk governance arrangements of Foresight Group Holdings Limited, an investment management business with activities across private markets, renewable energy, sustainable infrastructure, land and water investments, technology and growing businesses, and listed investment trusts.

I reviewed the organisation's reported risk management arrangements to understand how risk ownership, oversight, reporting and independent assurance are structured.

The assessment focuses on the Three Lines of Defence model and its relationship with the wider risk management framework, including risk appetite, Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA), risk reporting, escalation and third-party cybersecurity risk.

The objective was to examine how responsibilities are distributed across business functions, risk and compliance teams, and independent assurance, and how these arrangements support effective risk management.

## 2. Organisational Context

Foresight Group operates in investment management, where its business activities expose it to several categories of risk.

Based on the organisation's activities, relevant risk areas include:

- Cybersecurity and information security risk.
- Financial and market risk.
- Regulatory and compliance risk.
- Operational risk.
- Third-party risk.
- Data confidentiality and integrity risk.

Understanding the organisation's operating environment is important when assessing its risk management arrangements. Different business activities create different risk exposures, and responsibility for managing those risks needs to be clearly established.

## 3. Three Lines of Defence Model

I examined how the Three Lines of Defence model separates operational risk ownership, specialist risk oversight and independent assurance.

### First Line: Business Functions and Operations

**Primary responsibility: Own and manage risks arising from day-to-day business activities.**

Business departments and operational functions are closest to the activities that create risk. They are responsible for maintaining appropriate controls, following policies and procedures, identifying risks and escalating incidents.

Responsibilities include:

- Maintaining an appropriate control environment.
- Following established risk policies and procedures.
- Identifying and assessing risks arising from business activities.
- Contributing to risk identification and scoring.
- Reporting and escalating incidents and losses.
- Providing information for risk reporting.

**Practical application:**

Consider a finance department employee with access to confidential investment information.

The department is responsible for ensuring that access is appropriate, employees follow security requirements, and unusual access or suspected information disclosure is reported.

The underlying operational risk remains with the business function. The GRC or Risk team may provide guidance and oversight, but this does not automatically transfer risk ownership.

### Second Line: Risk and Compliance

**Primary responsibility: Provide oversight, guidance, monitoring and challenge.**

The second line provides specialist risk and compliance support to business functions and monitors how risks are being managed.

Responsibilities identified in the case study include:

- Implementing and monitoring risk policies.
- Identifying, scoring and prioritising risks with input from the first line.
- Maintaining risk reporting arrangements.
- Advising on regulatory developments.
- Analysing losses and incidents.
- Monitoring investment limits.
- Supporting risk escalation and management oversight.

**Practical application:**

Where a business function manages access to confidential investment information, the Risk and Compliance function may review the adequacy of its access controls.

This could involve examining whether permissions are appropriate, whether excessive privileges exist, whether access reviews are performed and whether identified control weaknesses are addressed.

The second line provides oversight and challenge while the business function retains responsibility for managing its operational risks.

### Third Line: Independent Assurance

**Primary responsibility: Independently assess the adequacy and effectiveness of governance, risk management and controls.**

The third line provides independent assurance over the organisation's risk management arrangements.

The case study identifies independent oversight activities that include:

- Verifying the adequacy of the Group Risk Framework.
- Independently challenging the adequacy and effectiveness of actions taken by the Risk Committee.

**Practical application:**

An appropriately independent assurance function may examine whether access controls are designed appropriately and operating effectively.

Evidence and testing could include:

- Reviewing access control policies.
- Examining access review records.
- Testing user permissions.
- Reviewing evidence of remediation.
- Reporting findings and recommendations.

The purpose is to provide an independent assessment rather than relying solely on the teams responsible for operating the controls.

## 4. Comparison of the Three Lines

| Line | Primary Responsibility | Key Question |
|---|---|---|
| First Line | Operational risk ownership and control management | Are we managing our risks? |
| Second Line | Risk oversight, monitoring and challenge | Are risks being managed appropriately? |
| Third Line | Independent assurance | Are the risk management arrangements effective? |

The distinction is important because risk ownership, oversight and independent assurance are separate responsibilities.

## 5. Risk Management Framework

I also reviewed the key components of the organisation's risk management framework.

| Concept | Application |
|---|---|
| Inherent Risk | Risk exposure before considering the effect of relevant controls. |
| Risk Mitigation | Actions or controls used to reduce risk exposure. |
| Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA) | A process through which business areas assess their risks and the controls used to manage them. |
| Risk Taxonomy | A structured classification of different categories of risk. |
| Risk Register | A record used to document, assess and track identified risks. |
| Risk Appetite | The level and types of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives. |
| Risk Escalation | Reporting significant risks, incidents or breaches to the appropriate management level. |

### Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA)

The case study highlights the first line's involvement in identifying and assessing inherent risks alongside existing controls and their proven or expected mitigating effects.

For example, consider the possibility of an employee accidentally sending confidential investment information to the wrong recipient.

| Assessment Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Risk Scenario | Confidential investment information is sent to an unauthorised recipient. |
| Inherent Risk | Unauthorised disclosure of confidential information. |
| Existing Control | Email Data Loss Prevention (DLP). |
| Control Assessment | Evaluate whether the DLP control is appropriately implemented and operating effectively. |
| Residual Risk | The remaining exposure after considering the effectiveness of the control. |

The second line can provide oversight and challenge the business area's assessment.

This illustrates how RCSA connects risk identification, control assessment and residual risk evaluation.

## 6. Risk Appetite and Governance

Risk appetite defines the types and levels of risk an organisation is willing to accept or avoid while pursuing its business objectives.

The case study identifies Foresight's Risk Appetite Statement as a mechanism for communicating the Group's approach to risk acceptance and avoidance. It also notes that risk appetite is reviewed annually and includes information security risk.

For an investment management organisation, different activities may have different risk tolerances.

For example:

- The organisation may accept a certain level of market risk in pursuit of investment returns.
- It may have a very low appetite for unauthorised access to confidential investor information.

Risk appetite provides a basis for determining whether identified risks fall within acceptable boundaries or require further treatment, escalation or management approval.

### Governance and Accountability

The case study also examines the relationship between the Board, Executive Committee and Risk function.

- **Board of Directors:** Retains ultimate oversight and accountability for the Group's risk framework.
- **Executive Committee:** Has delegated responsibility for risk management.
- **Risk function:** Supports risk oversight, reporting, monitoring and escalation.

This demonstrates how risk governance establishes accountability and supports management decision-making.

## 7. Cybersecurity Risk Considerations

The case study identifies cybersecurity and third-party risk as relevant areas for an investment management organisation.

One important consideration is the cybersecurity exposure created by external service providers.

A supplier with access to company systems or sensitive information may introduce vulnerabilities that affect the organisation.

### Illustrative Third-Party Risk Scenario

A technology service provider has access to Foresight's systems but does not maintain adequate access controls.

Potential consequences could include:

- Unauthorised access to company systems.
- Exposure of confidential business or investment information.
- Compromise of connected systems.
- Business disruption.
- Regulatory or reputational consequences.

### Application of the Three Lines

| Line | Role in Managing the Scenario |
|---|---|
| First Line | The business function responsible for the supplier manages the relationship and ensures agreed controls are implemented. |
| Second Line | Risk and Compliance assesses exposure, challenges the response, monitors remediation and escalates significant risks. |
| Third Line | Independent assurance evaluates whether third-party risk management arrangements are effective. |

This illustrates how risk ownership, oversight and independent assurance work together in managing third-party cybersecurity exposure.

## 8. Key GRC Observations

The review highlights several important considerations for effective risk governance:

**Risk ownership and accountability**

Business functions remain responsible for managing risks arising from their activities, even when specialist risk teams provide support and oversight.

**Control effectiveness**

The existence of a documented control does not establish that it is operating effectively. Evidence and appropriate testing are necessary to assess its performance.

**Independent assurance**

Independent assurance provides an additional level of scrutiny over governance, risk management and control arrangements.

**Risk appetite**

Risk appetite helps management determine which exposures are acceptable and which require mitigation, escalation or further action.

**Third-party risk**

External service providers can introduce cybersecurity exposure through their access to organisational systems and information.

## 10. Project Scope and Limitations

This is a desk-based GRC case study informed by the Foresight Group information and annual-report material reviewed as part of the exercise.

The analysis examines reported risk governance arrangements and applies them to illustrative cybersecurity risk scenarios.

It does not constitute an independent audit, penetration test, control effectiveness assessment or verification of Foresight Group's actual cybersecurity environment.
